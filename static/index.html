    Cybersecurity Safety Hub & Playbooks

    Comprehensive reference guidelines, incident response checklists, and interactive phishing simulation.

    🎣
    1. Phishing & Social Engineering Red Flags
    Cognitive bias manipulation & urgency triggers
    Phishing adversaries exploit emotional panic (account suspension, legal subpoenas, unpaid fines) or greed (lottery prizes, crypto airdrops) to force rapid, uncritical clicks.
    • Artificial urgency deadlines ("within 2 hours", "immediate response required") are primary signals of malicious intent.
    • Generic greetings ("Dear valued customer") instead of your registered legal name.
    • Demands to settle unexpected fines via gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency.
    ✉️
    2. Safe Email Practices & Header Verification
    Detecting domain spoofing and lookalike characters
    Always verify the technical email address behind the display name. Attackers configure display names like "PayPal Support" from disposable lookalike addresses.
    • Check the exact domain: support@paypal.com is genuine; support@paypa1-update.xyz is fraudulent.
    • Be vigilant for homoglyphs (e.g. Cyrillic 'a' or numeral '1' substituted for latin characters).
    • Verify SPF, DKIM, and DMARC alignment status in email headers.
    🔗
    3. Suspicious Links, Shorteners & Masked Destinations
    How attackers bypass domain reputation checks
    Links in SMS (smishing) and messaging apps frequently utilize URL shorteners (bit.ly, tinyurl) or raw IP addresses to obscure hostile landing sites.
    • Never click bare IP URLs (e.g., http://192.168.1.1/...) sent in unexpected messages.
    • Expand shortened URLs using link inspection tools before navigating.
    • Look for suspicious top-level domains (.xyz, .top, .buzz, .click, .loan).
    🛡️
    4. OTP & Multi-Factor Authentication Defense
    Preventing session hijacking and 2FA bypass
    One-Time Passwords (OTPs) are the final barrier preventing unauthorized account takeovers. Official institutions will NEVER ask for your OTP over phone, chat, or email.
    • If you receive an unsolicited OTP, your password may already be compromised. Change it immediately.
    • Beware of "MFA Fatigue" attacks where attackers trigger dozens of push approval alerts.
    • Prefer hardware FIDO2 security keys (YubiKey) or authenticator apps over SMS 2FA.
    🚨
    5. Incident Recovery Protocol (If You Clicked)
    Immediate remediation steps after accidental exposure
    Time is critical if you have clicked a suspicious link or entered credentials on a deceptive portal. Follow this structured protocol:
    • Step 1: Reset account passwords immediately from a separate, trusted device.
    • Step 2: Terminate all active browser sessions & revoked authorized tokens.
    • Step 3: Alert your bank / credit card company if financial data was disclosed.
    • Step 4: Run an offline antivirus/EDR scan for dropped malware or keyloggers.
